## 1. Purpose

This package contains the derived, aggregate data behind the tables, figures and numerical statements of the article and of its Supplementary Text S1: main-text Tables 1–8, A1 and B1 and Figures 1–11, and the numbers quoted in the text (main-text Sections 2–3, Appendices A–B, and Text S1 Sections S1–S5). README Section 5 maps every file to the manuscript elements it supports. README Section 6 indexes the files by table and figure, and README Section 7 explains the columns.

**Numbering.** Section numbers with the prefix S (for example Section S3.3) refer to Supplementary Text S1; all table and figure numbers, and section numbers without the prefix, refer to the main text (Tables A1 and B1 are in Appendices A and B). Six file names (`10_fig4a_…`, `10_fig4b_…`, `10_fig5b_…`, `13_fig6a_…`, `19_fig10_…`, `20_table4_…`) and a few text cells inside the CSV files carry the figure and table numbers used by the pipeline; with the restructured manuscript these coincide with the current numbers (README Section 6).

All 64 CSV files were produced by the seeded analysis pipeline of the article (Section 2.2) in its final run of 27 September 2026. Every table cell and every figure panel of the article and of Text S1 can be traced to a file of this package (README Section 6). The two-digit prefix of a file name (`03_`, `05_`, …, `21_`) identifies the pipeline module that produced the values.

## 2. Confidentiality

**No policyholder-level or personal data are included.**

Every row is one of the following: an aggregate over at least ten policies or claims, a result of a simulation on synthetic portfolios, a fitted model parameter, a test statistic, or a summary statistic. The package contains no identifiers such as policy, claim or person numbers. It also contains no minima or maxima of observed claims.

The raw policy, payment and case-reserve records are confidential administrative data of a Turkish insurer. They are protected by the Turkish Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 6698) and by the data provider's terms. See the Data Availability Statement of the article.

**Statistical disclosure control applied to this package:**

- **Body-type category.** Fewer than ten policies fall outside the modal category.
  - In `21_table1_covariates.csv`, the counts and shares of both body-type rows are replaced by `suppressed`, so the small count cannot be recovered by subtraction.
  - In `21_fsadj_full_regression.csv` (Table A1), every estimate cell of the body-type dummy row `body_type_STW` is replaced by `suppressed`. This covers the coefficient, standard errors, interval, VIFs and support counts. The dummy is identified by a single claim on one policy, so its coefficient is essentially that claim's residual and cannot be interpreted.
  - In `21_fsadj_diagnostics.csv`, the dummy's VIF and the R² of the "drop the dummy" sensitivity fit are also suppressed.
  - The regression specification itself is unchanged, so β_N equals the value in Table 2. Dropping the dummy leaves β_N at −0.0436 (row `sensitivity_drop_body_type_dummy`, `beta_N`).
- **Market premiums.** `18_adequacy.csv` (Figure 9) is trimmed to *relative*, portfolio-normalized loss ratios. The article does not interpret absolute market loss ratios, so the package contains no absolute market premium or market loss ratio. The same holds for `18_capital_premium_summary.csv`.
- **Top-coding.** The claim-share distribution by policy claim count in `21_claim_composition.csv` is top-coded at N ≥ 6. The policy claim-count distribution in `21_claim_count_distribution.csv` is top-coded at N ≥ 3. The observed claim-count distribution behind Figure 4b (`10_fig4b_claim_count_distribution.csv`) is given for exactly the plotted categories N = 0, …, 6 and N ≥ 7; every cell describes at least ten policies (N = 6: 31; N ≥ 7: 10). The fitted negative binomial and Poisson probabilities are given for every plotted category.
- **Binned claim data.** Every histogram bin of `10_fig4a_severity_histogram.csv` (Figure 4a) contains at least 54 claims, and every point of the empirical mean-excess curve in `10_fig5b_mean_excess.csv` (Figure 5b) averages at least 2,143 exceedances. The pipeline enforces a minimum of ten claims or policies per released cell. The histogram covers claims below the 99th percentile only, and the mean-excess thresholds are the 50th to 99th percentiles, so no individual large claim is disclosed.
- **Data-quality counts.** Two small counts remain. They are counts of records, not attributes of any policyholder:
  - 33 policies with zero earned exposure carry 5 severity claims (`21_cleaning_accounting.csv`, Table A1 flag on log exposure);
  - 285 claim files have nonpositive cumulative real payment.

## 3. Conventions

| Item | Convention |
|---|---|
| Monetary unit | Claim payments, case reserves, reserves, losses and premiums are in **real Turkish lira (TRY) at December-2018 prices**, deflated with monthly CPI. **Insured vehicle value is nominal** (policy-year TRY) in Table 1, Table 1 (Panel B) and Table A1. `21_table1_covariates.csv` and `21_portfolio_by_uw_year.csv` also give it at December-2018 prices, labelled as a sensitivity. `21_severity_summary.csv` reports claim severity at both real and nominal prices. |
| Simulation outputs | `05_*` files are in the simulated monetary units of the stylized generator (lognormal severity, meanlog 8, sdlog 1). `09_*` files are synthetic 200,000-policy portfolios drawn from the fitted marginals. `13_*` files are synthetic 100,000-policy portfolios in real TRY. `19_fig10_reserve_histogram.csv` counts 200,000 synthetic draws from the fitted lognormal predictive distribution of the reserve. |
| Rank correlation | Kendall's τ-b (tie-corrected), computed with SciPy on the raw tied data. No jitter is applied to reported τ values. |
| Dependence intervals | Percentile intervals from a policy-cluster bootstrap (policies resampled with all their claims, B = 500), unless stated otherwise. |
| Axis codes | `A_count_sum` is the naive count vs aggregate loss construction (B1). `B_count_average` is the naive count vs mean severity construction (B2). `FS_clean` is policy claim count vs individual claim severity at claim level. `FS_adj` is the covariate-adjusted log-severity regression coefficient β_N. `lag_clean` is settlement lag vs claim severity at claim level. Some files use the short forms `FS` and `lag`. |
| Capital changes (13) | `X_change_pct = (X_independent / X_dependent − 1) × 100`. A positive value means that assuming independence gives the higher figure (Table 5). K = VaR − E(S) is the capital distance. |
| Positive control (09) | `(Q99.5 independent / Q99.5 dependent − 1) × 100` at policy level. A negative value means that independence understates the quantile. |
| Percent vs fraction | Changes, effects and proportions whose names end in `_pct` are in percent. Two exceptions: `loading_pct` in `18_principle_differentiation.csv` is a fraction, and `MDE_80pct` denotes the MDE at 80% power. Quantities named `share`, `ratio` or `relative_change` are fractions. |
| Pricing split | Fit 2014–2015 (356,846 policies), validation 2016 (166,090), test 2017–2018 (295,853). All models, GLMs and boosted models alike, are fitted on 2014–2015; numeric covariates are standardized with the 2014–2015 means and SDs, applied unchanged to 2016 and 2017–2018; the model carried forward is the one with the highest 2016 validation Gini (two-stage GBM). A sensitivity refits the GLMs on 2014–2016 (`14b_train_window_sensitivity.csv`; split sizes in `14b_pricing_summary.csv`). |
| Pricing target | The per-policy loss modelled in module 14b and used for the realized losses of module 18 is the cumulative real paid amount plus the case reserves still open at the valuation date (December 2020), floored at zero. Case reserves of claims closed before that date are not added (README Section 9). |
| Seeds | All seeds, grids, thresholds and splits are fixed in the pipeline's single configuration file (global seed 20240517). |
| Number format | Unrounded double precision, exactly as written by the pipeline. The article rounds these values. `True`/`False` are Boolean flags. |
| Empty cells | Empty means not applicable. `suppressed` marks disclosure control (README Section 2). |

## 4. Provenance of the files

- **Pipeline CSV (48 files).** Each is a CSV output of the named module. Values are unchanged. Column names and categorical labels that were in Turkish have been translated into English, and a few columns were renamed for clarity (README Section 9). Exceptions:
  - `18_adequacy.csv` is trimmed (README Section 2).
  - `08_power_summary.csv` drops two legacy columns that exactly duplicated `SE_tau_H0_iid` and `MDE_80pct_iid`.
  - `07_conditional.csv` gains the tie fraction from the module's manifest.
  - In `09_recovery_table.csv`, the text interval `reject_rate_ci95` is split into two columns.
- **Manifest extract (16 files).** Values are copied verbatim from the machine-readable JSON manifest written by the same module in the same run. They are only reshaped into CSV; nothing is recomputed or rounded. These files cover numbers that the article quotes but that the module does not write to a CSV.
- **Figure-panel data and Table 4 ledger (6 of the 48 pipeline files, added in the final revision).** Modules 10, 13 and 19 write the aggregate data they plot in Figures 4a, 4b, 5b, 6a and 10 (`10_fig4a_*`, `10_fig4b_*`, `10_fig5b_*`, `13_fig6a_*`, `19_fig10_*`), and the synthesis module 20 writes the dependence-parameter ledger of Table 4 (`20_table4_dependence_ledger.csv`) with values copied from the manifests of modules 05, 06, 09, 11 and 13. These files were added without changing any estimate, seed or earlier output: after the re-run, every earlier output file of the pipeline was hash-identical to its previous version (JSON manifests gained only new keys), and the figures are unchanged.

## 7. Column glossary

Columns whose meaning follows from README Sections 3 and 5 are not repeated.

**Module 02–03**

- `02_cpi_sensitivity.csv`: `relative_change_real_incurred_losses` (fraction) when the CPI slope is shocked by −5% / +5%.
- `03_development_triangle.csv`: rows are accident years. Columns `0`–`6` hold the cumulative real paid amount at development year 0–6; empty cells are unobserved.
- `03_mack_se.csv`: `ultimate` (chain ladder), `mse` (Mack mean squared error), `se` = √mse, all in real TRY.
- `03_chain_ladder_summary.csv`: `quantity`, `value`, `note`. `unpaid_reserve_best_estimate` is the chain-ladder reserve (ultimate − paid; manifest key `ibnr`). `*_share_of_ultimate` values are fractions.

**Module 05 (stylized generator)**

- `05_bias_mechanical.csv`: `tau_true` is the Kendall τ of the latent dependence and `n` the portfolio size (policies). `tau_count_sum` = τ(N, S) (B1); `tau_per_claim` = clean per-claim τ. `n_rep` replications per cell (100, 33, 20 and 20 for n = 1,000, 5,000, 25,000 and 100,000); `_sd` are SDs across replications and `_mc_se` Monte Carlo SEs of the mean.
- `05_bias_ratio.csv`: `tau_count_average` = τ(N, S/N) (B2).
- `05_bias_compound.csv`: `block` 1–4 are four independent blocks of the same experiment at true τ = 0 (`tau_injected`). `legacy_tau_label` is the label under which each block was printed in the original package; it is not a dependence level. `VaR_leaky`/`VaR_clean` are mean 99.5% VaR with and without reuse of the count uniform. `spurious_inflation_pct` = (VaR_leaky / VaR_clean − 1) × 100. Block 1 gives the headline 276%.
- `05_bias_compound_reps.csv`: the same experiment per replication (`VaR_ratio` = leaky/clean; `inflation_pct`).
- `05_bias_summary.csv`: `quantity`, `value`, `ci_low`, `ci_high`, `note`. The B3 intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals over replications (B = 10,000).
- `05_recovery_ratio.csv`: `recovery_ratio` = `tau_per_claim` / `tau_true`, with its Monte Carlo SE.

**Modules 06–08 (dependence, power)**

- `06_dependence_summary.csv`: `kendall_tau_b` with `ci_low`/`ci_high` (policy-cluster percentile 95%). In row `FS_adj`, `kendall_tau_b` is intentionally empty; `beta_N` is the OLS coefficient of the claim count on log severity and `ci_low`/`ci_high` are its cluster CI. The percentage effect per additional claim is exp(β_N) − 1. The naive rows have no CI.
- `07_conditional.csv`: `tau_within_accident_year` = τ computed within accident years. `vuong_statistic` is cross-fitted; negative values favor independence. `reliable = False` marks the test as secondary evidence because `tie_fraction` is close to 1.
- `07_accident_year_summary.csv`: `n_claims` (severity sample) and within-year lag τ-b per accident year.
- `08_power_summary.csv`: `SE_tau_H0_iid` is the no-ties i.i.d. null SE and `SE_tau_cluster` the policy-cluster bootstrap SE. `MDE_80pct_*` are minimum detectable effects (two-sided 5%, power 80%). `design_effect` = (SE_cluster / SE_iid)². `power_at_obs` is the power at the observed τ.

**Module 09 (positive control)**

- `09_recovery_table.csv`: `tau_inject` is the injected latent τ; 30 replications of 200,000 policies per level.
  - Recovery: `tau_recovered_latent` (claim-level latent τ) and `tau_recovered_perclaim` (τ-b of count and severity), with SDs; `bias_latent`, `rmse_latent`.
  - Capital effects: `capital_change_total_pct` compares against severities drawn from the unconditional margin. `capital_change_margin_fixed_pct` compares against a permutation of the dependent arm's claim severities, which holds the claim-severity margin fixed (the dependence effect). SD/SE are across replications. `..._share_reps_material` is the share of replications beyond the 10% threshold.
  - Severity shift: `sev_mean_ratio` and `sev_median_ratio` = claim-level mean/median severity, dependent / unconditional.
  - Attenuation diagnostics: `tau_pop_latent` (all policies), `tau_claimant_policy` (claimant policies, one draw each) and their `ratio_*` to `tau_inject`.
  - Tie ceilings: `tie_ceiling_taub` = √(1 − Σq_k²) and `tie_ceiling_taua` = 1 − Σq_k².
  - `share_no_claim` = share of policies with no claim.
  - `reject_rate` with its Clopper–Pearson 95% CI.
- `09_recovery_criteria.csv`: `quantity`, `value`, `note`.

**Modules 10–12 (marginals, copulas)**

- `10_marginals.csv`: `ks` is the Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance; `xi`, `beta` are the GPD shape and scale; `variance_to_mean_ratio` refers to claim counts per policy.
- `10_gpd_threshold_grid.csv`: `quantile_q` of claim severity and `threshold_real_TRY`. `capital_proxy_pct_of_expected_loss` is the capital proxy under that threshold, in % of expected loss. `selected = True` marks the KS-minimizing threshold; for that row the file also gives the exceedance-bootstrap 95% CI of ξ, the tail index 1/ξ and its CI.
- `10_marginal_fit_parameters.csv`: parameters use the SciPy parametrizations (`lognorm(s, loc, scale)`, `nbinom(n = r, p)`).
- `10_fig4a_severity_histogram.csv`: `bin_left_real_TRY`/`bin_right_real_TRY` are the bin edges (80 equal-width bins from 0 to the 99th percentile of real claim severity, TRY 43,152). `n_claims` is the number of claims in the bin. `density_per_TRY1000` = n_claims / (214,283 × bin width) × 1,000 is the plotted density, normalized by the full sample so that it is comparable with the full-support density. `fitted_body_density_per_TRY1000_at_bin_mid` is the fitted lognormal body density at the bin midpoint, on the same scale.
- `10_fig4b_claim_count_distribution.csv`: `category` is the number of claims per policy. `n_policies` and `observed_share` are given for every plotted category 0–6 and `>=7`. `fit_prob` (negative binomial, ML) and `poisson_ref_prob` (Poisson with the sample mean) are the plotted model probabilities; for `>=7` they are survival probabilities.
- `10_fig5b_mean_excess.csv`: `threshold_u_real_TRY` is the `threshold_quantile_q` quantile of real claim severity; `n_exceedances` claims exceed it. `mean_excess_real_TRY` = E[Y − u | Y > u], `se_mean_excess` = sd(excess)/√n, and `band95_*` = mean excess ± 1.96 SE. `gpd_implied_mean_excess_real_TRY` = (β + ξ(u − u*))/(1 − ξ) for u ≥ u*, with u*, ξ and β from the selected row of `10_gpd_threshold_grid.csv`; the plotted line starts at u* with value β/(1 − ξ).
- `11_copulas.csv`: one row per axis and family. `theta` (MPLE); `theta_tau_inversion_start` (starting value); `loglik`, `aic`, `bic` (the independence copula has 0 on all three, so they equal ΔAIC/ΔBIC); `lambda_L`, `lambda_U` (tail dependence); `tau_implied` (Kendall τ implied by θ). For the selected family (`selected_by_aic = True`), the file adds the policy-cluster bootstrap CIs of θ and implied τ and the jitter mean/SD over 50 tie-breaking seeds. `theta_ci_*_mofn_legacy` is the superseded m-out-of-n interval (m = 5,000) of the original submission, kept for comparison only.
- `11_copula_selection.csv`: the table value is θ̂ with `theta_ci95_*_cluster`, a full-size policy-cluster bootstrap in which rank PITs and tie-breaking jitter are redrawn and the MPLE is refitted (B = 500). `bootstrap_share_independence_side` is the share of replicates on the independence side of `independence_value_of_theta`. `jitter50_*` summarize θ̂ over 50 jitter seeds.
- `12_gof.csv`: `p` is the Cramér–von Mises p-value against a simulated independence null; `p_holm` is Holm-adjusted; `reject_05` is the decision at 5%.

**Module 13 (capital simulation)**

- `13_capital.csv`: `alpha` is the confidence level. `*_dep` and `*_ind` are the dependent and independent arms. `ES_*` is the expected aggregate loss E(S). `K_*` = VaR − E(S). `*_change_pct` and `*_change_lo`/`*_change_hi` give the change and its paired-replication percentile bootstrap 95% CI (B = 1,000). The design is 100,000 policies and 20,000 paired replications in four seed blocks.
- `13_fig6a_exceedance_curves.csv`: `exceedance_prob` p runs over 500 log-spaced values from 0.5 to 1/20,000. `aggregate_loss_dependent_TRY` and `aggregate_loss_independent_TRY` are the (1 − p) empirical quantiles of the 20,000 paired aggregate losses of the main scenario (real TRY); the figure plots them in TRY million.
- `13_capital_summary.csv`: one row per scenario. `A_gauss_calibrated` is the Gaussian count-coupled link with ρ* calibrated so that the simulated claim-level τ-b equals τ̂. `B_empirical_rank` is the copula-free empirical conditional rank distribution by count class 1/2/3/4+.
  - `tau_b_claim_sim` (with SE) and `tau_b_claim_analytic` are the simulated and exact population claim-level τ-b.
  - `ks_Vcorr_p` is the KS p-value for uniformity of the margin-corrected claim-level uniforms.
  - `block*_` columns hold the per-seed-block changes.
  - `kupiec_*` is the split-sample coverage check. `split_p_exchangeable` is the exact p-value allowing for the estimated threshold.
  - `K995_change_pred_firstorder_pct` is the first-order analytic prediction.
- `13_capital_calibration.csv`: `quantity`, `tau_lat` (calibration point, empty for scalars), `value`, `note`.
- `13_capital_analytic_check.csv`: `variant` (D1 empirical moments with empirical or NB counts; D2 empirical-rank model; D3 Gaussian model and its ρ = 0 check), `quantity`, `count_class`, `value`. The ratios compare Var(S_i) and SD under dependence with their independence values.
- `13_tau_sweep.csv`: one row per τ_lat (20,000 paired replications each). `blocks_K995_change_pct` lists the four seed-block values, separated by `;`. `mean_Y_ratio_claim` is the claim-level mean-severity ratio, dependent / independent; it equals 1 up to Monte Carlo error because the margin is preserved.
- `13_tau_sweep_analytic_curve.csv`: first-order analytic `K_change_pred_pct` and `sd_ratio_pred` on a τ_lat grid, with the implied claim-level τ-b.

**Module 14b (pricing)**

- `14b_pricing_models.csv`:
  - `structure` ∈ {frequency-only, direct pure premium, two-stage, optional package}.
  - `test_deviance` is on each model's own scale; `test_deviance_metric` names that scale. `test_tweedie_dev_p1.5` is a common Tweedie (p = 1.5) deviance for all rows.
  - `gini` is the test-year (2017–2018) pure-premium Gini with its paired-bootstrap percentile CI (B = 500). `valid_gini` is the validation-year (2016) Gini, shown as "Valid. Gini" in Table 6; it is the criterion for the model carried forward and has no CI.
  - `delta_gini_vs_best_point` is the point difference to the selected model; `delta_gini_vs_best_bootmean` is the mean of the paired bootstrap differences. `delta_ci_low`/`delta_ci_high` are the 2.5/97.5 percentiles of the paired differences, and `delta_significant_5pct` is the corresponding test decision.
  - `nb_alpha` is the negative binomial dispersion.
  - `best_round` is the number of boosting rounds selected by early stopping: 124 for the frequency stage of the two-stage GBM (the severity stage uses 130; see `14b_model_specs.csv`). For XGBoost the value 62 is the zero-based best iteration, so 63 trees are used.
  - The model carried forward to calibration (Figure 8) and to the pricing decisions of module 18 is the one with the highest `valid_gini` among the direct pure-premium, two-stage and optional-package rows: the two-stage GBM (`valid_gini` = 0.2845, `gini` = 0.2519). Rows are ordered by test-year `gini`; the two-stage GLM has the highest test-year value (0.2528), but the test years are never used for selection, and the two models are statistically indistinguishable (`delta_ci_low` < 0 < `delta_ci_high`).
- `14b_factorial_effects.csv`: `effect_point` = Gini(minuend) − Gini(subtrahend), with paired-bootstrap CI (B = 500); `interaction` = learner effect (two-stage) − learner effect (direct).
- `14b_train_window_sensitivity.csv` (Text S1 Section S4.6): one row per contrast; `quantity` states minuend and subtrahend in words. Rows 1–4: for each GLM, `gini_fit_window` is the test-year Gini of the reported model (fitted on 2014–2015) and `gini_train_window` that of the same GLM refitted on 2014–2016 (522,936 policies; covariates standardized with that window's own parameters); `effect_point` = `gini_train_window` − `gini_fit_window`. Rows 5–6 (`model` = `learner_direct_vs_GLM_2014_2016`, `learner_twostage_vs_GLM_2014_2016`): `gini_fit_window` is the test-year Gini of the boosted model (fitted on 2014–2015), `gini_train_window` that of the refitted GLM, and `effect_point` = `gini_fit_window` − `gini_train_window`, the learner effect measured against the refitted GLM. `ci_lo`/`ci_hi` are paired-bootstrap percentile 95% CIs (B = 500) and `significant_95` the corresponding decision.
- `14b_calibration.csv`, `14b_recalibration.csv`: deciles of the predicted pure premium (test years). `actual_mean` and `pred_mean` are in real TRY per policy; `PO_ratio` = predicted / observed.
- `14b_calibration_slopes.csv`: three slope definitions (`loglog_claimants`, `decile_loglog`, `policy_linear`), each equal to 1 under perfect calibration, with bootstrap 95% CIs.
- `14b_tweedie_profile.csv`: per variance power `p`, `loglik_valid_profile` is the exact 2016 log-likelihood with φ profiled out by ML, and `delta_loglik_valid_profile` its difference to the maximum. It also gives the sensitivity criteria and IRLS convergence. `valid_deviance_p_old_criterion` is the superseded criterion (not comparable across p). `test_gini_refit_train_REPORT_ONLY` is for information only; it is not used for selection.
- `14b_pricing_summary.csv`: `quantity`, `value`, `note`. It covers the years and policy counts of each split (including the 2014–2016 window of the train-window sensitivity, `glm_refit_years`, `n_policies_glm_refit`), the covariate-scaling rule, the selected model with its validation-year Gini (`best_valid_gini`, the selection criterion) and test-year Gini (`best_gini`), the model with the highest test-year Gini (`highest_test_gini_model`, reported only), the Tweedie power selection (selected p, maximized log-likelihood, φ̂, alternative criteria, pre-R1 value), and the overall P/O and Gini before and after isotonic recalibration.
- `14b_model_specs.csv`: `spec_json` holds the full specification of each model as JSON: learner, structure, years, sample sizes, loss, early-stopping rule, trees selected, tree constraints, package parameters and versions.

**Module 18 (pricing decisions)**

- `18_adequacy.csv`: deciles of the predicted pure premium of the selected model (test years). `mean_predicted_loss` is in real TRY per policy. `rel_loss_ratio_*` = decile loss ratio / portfolio loss ratio of the same premium; values above 1 indicate relative under-pricing of the decile.
- `18_capital_premium_summary.csv`: `quantity`, `value`, `note`, for the 2017–2018 test portfolio. The capital proxy is q_0.995(S) − E(S) of the independent-sum compound-Poisson aggregate, computed by Cornish–Fisher with a Monte Carlo cross-check and its simulation SE. The file also gives the negative binomial sensitivities, the hurdle rate h, the expense ratio e, the RORAC-target premium and its loss ratios, the capital margin h·SCR and its share of premium, the mean CPI multiplier applied to recorded premiums, differentiation ratios and severity moments. Values labelled `share` are fractions; monetary values are in real TRY.
- `18_principle_differentiation.csv`: `mean_expected_loss` and `mean_office_premium` (real TRY per policy); `loading_pct` is the loading as a fraction (not a percentage) of the expected loss. The expected-value, variance, exponential and cost-of-capital principles coincide because the compound variance is proportional to the mean.
- `18_rorac_frontier.csv`: `premium` is the portfolio RORAC-target (technical) premium; `expected_loss_ratio` and `realized_loss_ratio` are loss ratios of that premium; `combined_ratio` adds the expense ratio.

**Module 19 (reserving)**

- `19_reserve_strategies.csv`: `margin_over_be` (TRY) and `ratio_to_be` are relative to the chain-ladder best estimate. For row `coc_margin`, `level` is the CoC rate (6%) and `reserve` is the technical provision BE + risk margin under standard Solvency II timing.
- `19_optimal_reserve.csv`: `penalty_ratio_cu_co` = c_u/c_o; `critical_fractile` = c_u/(c_u + c_o). `optimal_reserve` (lognormal) and `optimal_reserve_{gamma, weibull, invgauss, odp_bootstrap}` are the corresponding R*. `*_over_ln` and `pct_diff_*_vs_ln` are relative to the lognormal. `spread_max_over_min` is taken across the four parametric distributions. `exp_cost_per_unit_co` is the expected cost in units of c_o (lognormal).
- `19_distribution_sensitivity.csv`: `measure` ∈ {VaR, TVaR, newsvendor_Rstar, CoC_TP_cdf, CoC_TP_implied_cu_co}.
  - For VaR and TVaR, `level` is the confidence level. For newsvendor rows, `level` is c_u/c_o.
  - For the two `CoC_TP` rows, `level` holds the technical provision (TRY), and the distribution columns hold F(TP) or the implied c_u/c_o = F/(1 − F).
  - The four parametric distributions are moment-matched to the chain-ladder reserve and the Mack SE. `odp_bootstrap` is the England–Verrall over-dispersed Poisson bootstrap (5,000 replications, process error).
- `19_risk_margin.csv`: `timing` is `solvency2_standard` (used in the article) or `as_originally_submitted` (sensitivity).
  - Run-off rows: `t` indexes the run-off year. `expected_payment_P_k` is the expected chain-ladder payment in future year k = t, with P_0 = 0 and negative expected increments set to zero. `outstanding_O_t` is the expected outstanding amount at the end of year t. SCR_t = SCR_0 × O_t/O_0, where SCR_0 = VaR99.5 − BE. `RM_term` = CoC × SCR_t / (1 + r_f)^(t+1), with r_f = 0 because amounts are in constant prices.
  - Scalar rows (empty `t`): the risk margin `rm`, `technical_provision`, and the quantile and implied c_u/c_o of the technical provision under each distribution.
- `19_predictive_distributions.csv`: `distribution`, `parameter`, `value`, `note` (moment-matching method). The rows `figure10_histogram` hold the settings of the histogram in Figure 10.
- `19_fig10_reserve_histogram.csv` (Figure 10): 60 equal-width bins from 0 to the 99.9% lognormal quantile, in TRY million as plotted; `n_draws` counts the 200,000 seeded draws in each bin. The 197 draws beyond the axis are reported in `19_predictive_distributions.csv` (`draws_beyond_x_max`).

**Module 20 (synthesis)**

- `20_audit_gates.csv`: `condition`, `passed`, `description`. All eleven conditions pass (`n_failed_conditions` = 0).
- `20_table4_dependence_ledger.csv` (Table 4): `block` ∈ {empirical_estimate, fitted_copula_parameter, capital_simulation_input, prescribed_simulation_value}. `value`, `ci_low`, `ci_high` are copied verbatim from the manifest key named in `source_manifest_key`; `origin` and `used_for` describe where each value comes from and where it is used. The sweep appears as pairs of rows: the prescribed `sweep_tau_lat` and the simulated claim-level τ_b it produces. The file is the full ledger. Table 4 of the article is a compact version: it merges `origin` and `used_for` into one column, gives the simulated sweep τ_b only as its range (−0.27 to +0.27), and omits five rows that are reported elsewhere: `naive_B1_count_sum_tau_b`, `naive_B2_count_average_tau_b` and `tau_b_lag_claim_level` (Table 2), `copula_lag_Joe_lambda_U` (Table 3) and `calibration_target_tau_hat` (equal to τ̂_FS). In `origin` and `used_for`, "Table 3" denotes the copula table (Table 3), "Figure 3" the positive control (Figure 3) and "Figure 6b" the sweep panel (Figure 6b); these labels coincide with the current numbering (README Section 6).

**Module 21 (descriptive statistics, covariate-adjusted regression of Section Appendix A, tests)**

- `21_table1_covariates.csv`: `block` ∈ {numeric, categorical, sensitivity (not Table 1)}. Numeric rows give `mean`, `sd` (ddof = 1), `p5`, `median`, `p95`; categorical rows give `count` and `share` of the 818,789 policies. Gender levels are male, female and corporate. Corporate policyholders are recorded with insured age 0; the rows `insured_age` document this convention. Body type is suppressed (README Section 2).

- `21_claims_by_accident_year.csv`: claim files with positive cumulative real payment (the severity sample, 214,283), all linked files (214,568) and nonpositive-payment files (285), by accident year.
- `21_claim_count_distribution.csv`: policy-level N = distinct linked claim files per policy.
- `21_claim_composition.csv`:
  - `q_k` is the share of severity claims on policies with N = k (`count` = claims).
  - `tie_bound` rows hold the maximal τ-a and τ-b given the count ties.
  - `multi_claim` rows hold the shares of claims and of real losses on policies with N ≥ 2.
  - `composition_effect` rows give six variants of the expected-loss shift implied by β_N; `definition` gives each formula.
- `21_severity_summary.csv`: `claim_set` S1 is the primary severity sample (positive cumulative real payment), S2 has positive nominal payment and S3 contains all linked files. `price_basis` is `real_Dec2018` or `nominal`.
- `21_fsadj_full_regression.csv`: OLS of log real severity on the claim count N, accident-year dummies (2015–2019 vs 2014), log earned exposure, standardized vehicle age, insured age, horsepower, displacement and insured vehicle value (nominal), gender dummies (female, corporate vs male) and the body-type dummy.
  - `beta`, `se_cluster_boot` (policy-cluster bootstrap SD, B = 500) and `ci_lo`/`ci_hi` (percentile 95%); `se_ols_iid` is the classical SE.
  - `vif_full_design` and `vif_reduced_numeric_design` are VIFs in the full design and in the design with only the count, log exposure and five numeric covariates.

It covers fit statistics, both VIF designs, value/horsepower/displacement correlations (policy and claim level) and β_N under three sensitivity fits, plus a consistency check with module 06. In `note`, "Table A1" denotes the regression table (Table A1 of Appendix A).

## 8. Reproducibility

All files are produced by the seeded analysis pipeline of the article. It consists of 23 modules (00–21 plus 14b) controlled by a single configuration file, and one command (`python run_pipeline.py`) regenerates every table and figure from the confidential administrative files. Each module writes a machine-readable JSON manifest; the manifest-extract files of this package are taken from those manifests.

The final run used Python 3.12.11 with numpy 2.4.3, pandas 3.0.2, scipy 1.17.1, matplotlib 3.10.8, statsmodels 0.14.6, lightgbm 4.6.0 and xgboost 3.2.0. The boosting packages run single-threaded with a fixed seed. The pipeline, its pinned environment and execution instructions will be released in the public repository accompanying the article. Full end-to-end reproduction requires authorized access to the confidential raw files.
